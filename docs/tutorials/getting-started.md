# A guided tour of the dashboard

*Estimated time: 10 minutes. No prior knowledge needed.*

This tutorial walks you through the Linguist Corpus Analyzer dashboard for the first time. By the end, you will have explored how a single political term — *Nakba* — is used across two news outlets, and you will know where to click for everything else.

## What you are looking at

The dashboard analyses **19,744 articles** published between **May 2009 and December 2025** by two sister outlets:

- **+972 Magazine** — English-language, 8,440 articles
- **Local Call (Mekomit)** — Hebrew-language, 11,304 articles

The two outlets share an editorial line and frequently cross-publish, but they write in different languages for different audiences. The dashboard counts how often each of 19 political terms appears in each article, then lets you slice the data by date, author, and author category.

## Step 1 — Pick a term

On the dashboard, find the **Term** selector and choose **Nakba**.

The chart below redraws to show how often the word appears, week by week, across the full corpus. You should see spikes around May (Nakba Day) and during periods of acute violence.

## Step 2 — Read the timeline

The timeline shows two lines, one per outlet. The y-axis is **occurrences per 1,000 words**, not raw counts — this makes the two outlets comparable even though they publish at different volumes.

> **Tip:** Drag horizontally on the chart to zoom into a date range. Double-click to reset.

## Step 3 — Filter by author category

Open the **Author status** filter in the sidebar. Authors are grouped into seven categories based on ethnonational identity and place of residence — see the [reference page on author statuses](../reference/data-and-terms.md#author-statuses) for the full breakdown.

Select **Palestinian citizen of Israel** and observe how the timeline changes. The same term, used by a different group of writers, can produce a very different trend line.

## Step 4 — Drill down to articles

Scroll past the chart to the **Results table**. Each row is a single article with its date, author, title, and a hit count. Click a row's URL to read the original article on the publisher's site.

## Step 5 — Compare a second term

Return to the term selector and choose **Apartheid** or **Occupation**. You will quickly see that some terms are evenly distributed across outlets, while others belong almost entirely to one.
